No account means no live data. We think that is the right way round.

Spotify’s API caps an app at five users total. Using it would mean logins, a server, and a waiting list. Reading your file instead means unlimited people, no account, and a history that goes back further than any API can reach.

Get your file →

Step one: ask Spotify for your data.

You do this once. Spotify emails a link — usually within a few hours, sometimes a few days. There are two downloads on that page and only one is right.

  1. Go to spotify.com/account/privacy and log in.
  2. Scroll to “Download your data”.
  3. Tick the right box. This is the part everyone gets wrong.
  4. Click Request data, then confirm from the email Spotify sends.
  5. Wait for the second email with your link, then come back and upload.

The Spotify app can’t request your data. You need a browser — your phone’s browser is fine.

  1. Open your browser — Chrome, Safari, Samsung Internet. Not the Spotify app.
  2. Go to spotify.com/account/privacy and log in.
  3. Tap “Desktop site” if the page looks cut off.
  4. Scroll to “Download your data” and tick Extended streaming history — not “Account data”.
  5. Request it, confirm by email, then upload the zip. No need to unzip.

        Privacy

        Alpha 1.0 · last updated 30 August 2026

        The short version

        Your Spotify file never leaves your device. There is no account, no login and no server holding your listening history. You can verify this yourself: open your browser’s developer tools, go to the Network tab, and upload your file. Nothing containing your data is sent anywhere.

        What happens to your file

        When you upload, the file is read in your browser’s memory and turned into statistics. It is not copied, stored or transmitted. Closing or refreshing the tab discards everything — which is why you have to upload again each visit.

        What does leave your browser

        Two things, and neither one carries anything about you:

        • Album artwork. For albums currently visible on your screen, we ask Deezer's public catalogue for a cover image, sending only an artist name and an album name. We never send play counts, timestamps, totals, rankings, or anything describing how much you listened. Switch artwork off and this stops entirely.
        • Our own contact status. The small contact chips ask Lanyard's public endpoint whether a member of our team is online on Discord. That request fetches our status and sends nothing whatsoever about you — no identifier, no listening data, not even a note that you visited. Blocking it changes nothing except that the chip shows GitHub instead.

        Cover results are cached in your browser’s local storage so the same album is not looked up twice. That cache stays on your device.

        What we store on your device

        • Your light or dark theme preference.
        • The album-art cache described above.
        • Whether you have dismissed the ad-blocker notice this session.

        Nothing here identifies you, and none of it is sent to us.

        Analytics and ads

        This alpha runs no advertising and no analytics. If advertising is added later it will be disclosed here before it ships, and it could never be based on your listening — we do not receive it.

        Children

        Longplay is not directed at children under 13 and we knowingly collect nothing from anyone, of any age.
